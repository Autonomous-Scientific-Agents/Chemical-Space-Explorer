What is counted?

Neutral, closed-shell, connected molecules with implicit hydrogens, counted as constitutional isomers (no stereoisomers; resonance forms count once). A molecule must pass basic rules: standard valences, bond orders ≤ 3, GDB-style ring-strain filters (no small-ring triple bonds, cyclopropenes or anti-Bredt alkenes) and heteroatom filters (no O–O, N–O or halogen–heteroatom bonds, enols or hemiacetals). These are candidate structures, not experimentally verified compounds.

How is the number estimated?

Counts come from chemuniverse. Every molecular graph is generated exactly once (canonical augmentation) and counted exactly up to 6 heavy atoms. Larger sizes, up to 12, are estimated by stratified sampling of the generation tree. Beyond that the curve is extrapolated from the computed growth rate and is illustrative only. It never drops below the exact alkane counts. GDB-13/17/20 are shown for comparison; they use stricter filters, so they sit below these counts.
